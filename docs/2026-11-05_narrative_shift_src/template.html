From “not MYC” to “not proliferation”

Until late October the human arm said that the BCL-2 family signature tracked OXPHOS but not MYC. The confounder was proliferation, carried inside the MYC activity score. With proliferation in the model, MYC activity goes with the signature in the same direction as OXPHOS, and OXPHOS and proliferation run opposite ways, on the BCL-2 genes and on chemotherapy response.

Exploratory and post hoc. An explainer for co-authors, not a manuscript figure. Every number is read from a verified saved result; the sources are listed at the foot.

Retired in October

The apoptotic signature tracks OXPHOS but not MYC.
The association with MYC activity was lost after adjustment for OXPHOS.

The narrative of record, 22 October: abstract, Fig. 4 human paragraphs and closing paragraph.

Current text, v6 (4 November)

…a BCL-2 family signature that tracked OXPHOS at fixed proliferation showed BCL-xL rising more steeply than PUMA. MYC activity, which played no part in defining it, went with the same signature when measured with scores least correlated with proliferation.
Proliferation, although correlated with OXPHOS, was not part of this configuration and went the other way on chemotherapy response.

Abstract and closing sentences, as ruled on 3 and 4 November.

Why the old line failed

  • A result was misread. In September, E11 showed that conditioning on OXPHOS removes MYC’s ordering of 44 death-machinery genes (from 0.187 to −0.043 in TCGA). That ranks genes; it says nothing about tumours’ expression levels, which is how the text read it.
  • The comparison was unequal. The signature’s genes were chosen by their correlation with OXPHOS in these same cohorts, so “tracks OXPHOS” was guaranteed. “But not MYC” set an unconstrained number against it.
  • Proliferation was hidden in the MYC score. The reference MYC score correlates with proliferation at 0.70 and 0.61. At fixed OXPHOS and MYC activity, proliferation goes with lower signature expression in all 24 model cells (−0.22 to −0.38). In the raw correlation the two parts cancel (panel a); in the model they separate (panel b).

How the line changed

  1. Early SeptemberE11. MYC’s ordering of the death genes vanishes when conditioned on OXPHOS; read as “OXPHOS rather than MYC”.
  2. 22 OctoberThe Doc and E46. The text says the MYC association “was lost after adjustment for OXPHOS”, while the new 4g shows a MYC increment at both OXPHOS levels.
  3. 23 OctoberE47, one model with both. MYC activity is above zero at fixed OXPHOS in 4 of 8 primary cells on the reference score, and in 8 of 8 on the score least tied to proliferation. “Lost after adjustment” is withdrawn.
  4. 2 NovemberThe separation brief. “Not MYC” is retired. The line becomes OXPHOS against proliferation, the paper’s own argument from Fig. 1.
  5. 3 NovemberE48 and E49. BBC3 and BCL2L1 rise with OXPHOS and fall with proliferation; MYC activity’s coefficient on the signature is positive in all four blocks.
  6. 3–4 NovemberThe rulings and v6. The signature is introduced as selected at fixed proliferation. The evidence the selection could not have produced is BBC3 and BCL2L1, MYC activity and the effectors.

What still limits the wording

  • MYC’s positive coefficient is established on the two scores least tied to proliferation (E49). On the reference score E47’s verdict stays mixed, so the text names the scores and never says “independently of”.
  • Part of the signature’s proliferation coefficient can be inherited from how it was selected, up to about half in TCGA and a third in SCAN-B (ad hoc arithmetic, E49 H2). The text therefore carries the proliferation point on BBC3 and BCL2L1 (panel c), which the selection did not choose.
  • All models are main effects only. v6’s “so that their associations added” describes that arithmetic; no MYC × OXPHOS interaction was tested here.
