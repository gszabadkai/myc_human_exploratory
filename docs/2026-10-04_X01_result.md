# X01 result - where might a predictive marker around OXPHOS be?

**Option A: Claude Code wrote the script, the author sourced it.** Nothing was
rescored, `METABRIC_DATA.RData` was not reloaded, and nothing already in
`e41_metabric_ladder.rds` was refitted. Nothing was written to
`myc_human_validation` (`d3ac60e`) or `myc_mouse`.

## 0. Verification

Two redirected dry runs agreed on 19 of 19 non-timestamp elements; the digests
went into `68c0f4c` **before** the author's run; the author's run matched
**19 of 19**. `E41` was re-sourced first, and its **26 pre-existing elements came
back byte-identical** with only `$frame` new - so `51465b6` changed no number,
as it claimed.

Three of six PAM50 strata were fittable: **LumA, LumB and Her2**. Normal-like
and Basal failed on **parameter count rather than event scarcity** - Normal-like
has 38 events, ample for a cheap spine, and fails only because `treatment`
carries eight levels. No reduced spine was fitted.

## 1. LumB carries the pooled signal; LumA does not

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| LumA | 696 | 142 | m0 | 1.0315 | [0.8675, 1.2265] | no | 0.725 |
| LumA | 696 | 142 | m1 | 0.9954 | [0.8303, 1.1932] | no | 0.960 |
| **LumB** | 471 | 173 | **m0** | **0.7972** | **[0.6772, 0.9385]** | **yes** | 0.00649 |
| **LumB** | 471 | 173 | **m1** | **0.7532** | **[0.6343, 0.8945]** | **yes** | 0.00123 |
| Her2 | 113 | 53 | m0 | 0.8371 | [0.5929, 1.1818] | no | 0.312 |
| Her2 | 113 | 53 | m1 | 0.7481 | [0.5019, 1.1151] | no | 0.154 |

E41's pooled ER-positive `m1` was **HR 0.8962 [0.8034, 0.9996]**. **LumB sits
below it and is the only stratum whose interval excludes 1. LumA sits at the
null.** Under the `ER.Expr` repartition LumB holds (0.7750 [0.6544, 0.9176]) and
LumA stays flat (0.9922) - **an internal check on the same cohort
repartitioned, never replication.** Her2 **drops out of fittability entirely**
under `ER.Expr` (4.8 events per parameter).

**The scatter, which must be read beside this.** The three estimates span
**0.286** on the log-hazard scale and **only 2 of 3 intervals contain the
pooled value** - LumB's does not. Caution 1 applies: scatter of this size is
expected from sampling variation alone, and no subtype is a lead merely for
being the largest.

**And Her2 scores three of three on the false-lead signature** the brief
described in advance: the **most protective point estimate** (-0.2902), the
**fewest events** of the three fitted (53), the **highest VIF** (1.649), and it
**disappears under the other ER call**. It is reported and is not a lead.

## 2. Criterion 1's prediction failed

Criterion 1 asked for mechanistic coherence and named its expectation:
*"LumA is the most differentiated and most oxidative."* **LumA is flat at both
rungs.** The subtype that carries the association is **LumB** - the more
proliferative luminal subtype, and the opposite of the offered mechanism.

**This is caution 3 happening again.** E42 measured
`rho(OX, subtype2) = -0.054` and refuted a plausible story about luminal
tumours being more oxidative. The same kind of story was offered here, for the
same reason, and the data again declined it. **Two mechanisms proposed, two
refused.** That is now a pattern in this arm's reasoning about respiration and
differentiation state, and it is worth more than either individual refutation.

**No replacement mechanism is offered here.** Writing one after seeing which
subtype won would be the third instance of the same error.

## 3. Criterion 3 was the wrong criterion for a biomarker purpose

Criterion 3 said: *"A subtype where m0 is null and m1 is protective is a better
lead than one where both are protective, because the first shows the adjustment
doing work."*

**That is the right criterion for the E-line's question and the wrong one for
this note's.** The E-line asks a mechanistic question - does respiration carry
information beyond proliferation - and for that, a coefficient that appears only
after adjustment is exactly the interesting case. **This note asks whether a
marker could be built.** For that purpose the criterion inverts, for two
reasons that pull together rather than apart:

1. **An unadjusted association is the deliverable.** A marker that predicts
   outcome only after a 318-gene proliferation score has been measured and
   conditioned on is a worse clinical instrument than one that predicts
   outcome on its own, not a better one. Criterion 3 as written **selects for
   the absence of the thing a biomarker needs.**
2. **Surviving adjustment is evidence of INCREMENTAL value, not of validity.**
   Proliferation is already clinically available - grade, Ki67, the commercial
   recurrence scores. So adjustment-survival answers "does this add anything to
   what the clinic already has?", which is a second and different question from
   "does this predict?".

**The biomarker-relevant pair is therefore the reverse of criterion 3**: an
association present *unadjusted*, which **also** survives proliferation
adjustment. On that pair the ranking changes:

| subtype | unadjusted association (m0) | survives adjustment (m0 -> m1) | criterion 3 as written | the biomarker pair |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **LumB** | **yes**, 0.7972 excl 1 | **yes**, moves to 0.7532 | **scored WORST** | **scored BEST** |
| Her2 | no, CI spans 1 | n/a - m1 also spans 1 | closest to the pattern | neither limb |
| LumA | no | no, both null | not met | neither limb |

**So criterion 3 ranked LumB last on the very property that makes it the only
candidate worth the name.** The `m0 -> m1` step for LumB is **-0.0568** - small,
and that is the point: the association is not proliferation in disguise *and*
does not need proliferation measured to appear.

**None of this makes LumB a finding.** It is one subtype of one cohort, its
interval is the only one in the table excluding the pooled value, E42 13.2 still
forbids it, and caution 2's winner's-curse argument applies to it as much as to
anything else. **What changed is which criterion to apply, not what the estimate
is worth.** Criterion 3 is not wrong in general; it was imported from a
mechanistic frame into a biomarker survey where it means something different,
and that is the lesson.

## 4. The treatment reversal, and confounding by indication as its leading explanation

| treatment | n | events | chemo | HR | 95% CI | median follow-up (y) | |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| HT/RT | 585 | 161 | no | 0.8796 | [0.739, 1.047] | 7.15 | |
| HT | 388 | 122 | no | 0.8371 | [0.678, 1.033] | 7.23 | |
| NONE | 219 | 67 | no | 0.8279 | [0.624, 1.098] | 9.72 | **WARNED** |
| **CT/HT/RT** | **115** | **32** | **yes** | **1.3476** | **[0.848, 2.142]** | **5.08** | |
| RT | 166 | 32 | no | 0.8623 | [0.552, 1.347] | 11.63 | |

Every non-chemotherapy group lies in **0.828 to 0.880**. The one fittable
chemotherapy group sits at **1.348**. That is the sign reversal note section 4
hypothesised, and **no interval in the table excludes 1.**

**CONFOUNDING BY INDICATION IS THE LEADING EXPLANATION**, and the frame shows
three of its fingerprints without being able to quantify any of them:

1. **The chemotherapy group is the most proliferative.** Mean `PROLIF` is
   **-0.048** in CT/HT/RT against **-0.147** (HT/RT), **-0.186** (HT),
   **-0.330** (RT) and **-0.383** (NONE). **That IS the indication**: these
   patients received chemotherapy because their tumours were more
   proliferative. `PROLIF` is in the model, but adjusting for a variable that
   determined treatment allocation does not undo selection on it.
2. **Follow-up differs by a factor of 2.3** - 5.08 y in the chemotherapy group
   against 11.63 y in RT. Chemotherapy use rose across METABRIC's accrual, so
   this group is observed later and briefly. **E41 found proportional hazards
   failing for OX in every pooled breast-cancer-specific model**, so groups
   observed over different stretches of a time-varying hazard are not
   comparable, and the reversal could be a window effect rather than a
   treatment effect.
3. **The chemotherapy group is depleted of the subtype carrying the
   association.** LumB is **27.8%** of CT/HT/RT against **39.0%** of HT/RT,
   while Normal-like is **18.3%** against **9.2%**. PAM50 is in the spine, but
   adjustment across five levels on 32 events is weak.

**This cannot be tested from disk, and that is itself recorded.** The
indication covariates - `grade`, `size`, `lymph_nodes_positive`,
`age_at_diagnosis` - are in METABRIC's clinical table and **`E41` did not carry
them into its frame.** So the leading explanation for the only result matching
the note's hypothesis **cannot be weighed**. That gap is the second of the two
blocks behind the new standing requirement in
`docs/R_CODING_INSTRUCTIONS.md` section 3.

**What would be needed**, named here and not done: the reversal re-examined with
the indication covariates in the model, on a treatment contrast with enough
events to carry them - which METABRIC's ER-positive stratum does not have, at
**32 of 49 chemotherapy events fittable against 283 endocrine-only**.

## 5. What this licenses

**Nothing for the manuscript.** Note section 5 holds in full: no sentence, no
figure, no legend, no supplementary table, and no claim that any stratum
*shows* or *demonstrates* anything.

**For a future proposal, three things are now known cheaply** that were not
before, and all three are negative or cautionary:

- **The subtype that carries the ER-positive association is LumB, not LumA**,
  so a proposal resting on an oxidative-differentiated-luminal mechanism would
  be resting on the subtype where the association is absent.
- **The predictive-marker hypothesis cannot be surveyed in METABRIC.** The
  chemotherapy arms are too small and too confounded by indication, and the
  needed covariates are not even carried. **This redirects a proposal toward
  the prognostic hypothesis, or toward a cohort with a randomised or
  protocol-defined treatment assignment**, which is what the brief anticipated
  as the useful negative outcome.
- **The criterion set itself needed revising** before it could rank anything -
  see section 3.

## 6. Limitations

1. **Three of six PAM50 strata were never fitted.** Normal-like and Basal fail
   on parameter count, not event scarcity; NC has 6 patients.
2. **Her2 is fitted and flagged** at 6.6 events per parameter, and is not
   fittable at all under the other ER call.
3. **One treatment fit is not clean.** The NONE row is WARNED: a PAM50 level
   inside it has patients but zero events, so one spine coefficient is
   infinite. OX remains estimable. **The level was not dropped** - unlike an
   empty level, a zero-event level still contributes its patients to other
   levels' risk sets.
4. **The detectable-effect figures are Schoenfeld approximations** that ignore
   covariate adjustment, so the true detectable effects are larger than
   reported. **A null in a low-event subtype is uninformative, not absence.**
5. **SINGLE-INSTRUMENT.** METABRIC is an array; mitoPPS is unavailable. No
   coefficient is compared across cohorts as a raw value.
6. **One cohort, repartitioned twice.** The `ER.Expr` block is an internal
   consistency check on the same patients and is **not replication.**
7. **No interaction or effect-modification test was fitted anywhere**, and the
   event counts would not support one.
